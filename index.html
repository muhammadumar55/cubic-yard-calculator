How to Calculate Cubic Yards manually

If you want to double-check your order without a tool, the core formula is:

(Length in Feet × Width in Feet × Depth in Feet) ÷ 27 = Cubic Yards

Step-by-step

  1. Measure length and width in feet.
  2. Measure depth. If you measured depth in inches, convert to feet: depth (ft) = depth (in) ÷ 12.
  3. Multiply to get cubic feet: ft³ = L × W × D.
  4. Convert cubic feet to cubic yards by dividing by 27: yd³ = ft³ ÷ 27.

Example: 12 ft × 10 ft × 4 in deep → depth = 4/12 = 0.333 ft → ft³ = 12×10×0.333 ≈ 40 → yd³ = 40/27 ≈ 1.48 yd³.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

A cubic yard (yd³) is a unit of volume equal to 27 cubic feet. Think of a box that’s 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft. Bulk materials like mulch, gravel, topsoil, and concrete are commonly sold by the yard.

One cubic yard is 27 cu ft. Many 80-lb bags yield about 0.60 cu ft each (≈ 45 bags per yard), and many 60-lb bags yield about 0.45 cu ft each (≈ 60 bags per yard). Always confirm the yield printed on your bag.

At 3 inches (0.25 ft) deep, one cubic yard (27 cu ft) covers about 108 sq ft (27 ÷ 0.25). A common landscaping rule of thumb is about ~100 sq ft per yard at 3-inch depth.

Divide cubic feet by 27. Example: 54 cu ft ÷ 27 = 2 yd³.

Professional notes

  • Compaction: gravel and crushed stone often compact 10–15% depending on gradation.
  • Moisture: wet soil/sand can weigh significantly more than dry material.
  • Delivery: confirm maximum truck payload limits using the weight estimator.
